# Pricing — what to charge and how to defend it

## Never quote an hourly rate

An hourly rate invites comparison against the cheapest freelancer available, and it punishes you for
being fast. Once you've built the same system twice, the third one takes a fraction of the time — with
hourly billing that means earning less for delivering the same result sooner.

Price the outcome. If it saves them $2,000 a month, a $3,000 build is obviously worth it regardless of
whether it took you four days or fourteen.

When someone insists on an hourly number, give a range and immediately reframe:

> "It works out around $45–70 an hour depending on the work, but I quote fixed prices — you get a
> number that doesn't move and I carry the risk of it taking longer than I thought."

---

## Starting ladder

Offshore rates for a solo engineer, realistic for a UAE/US client base. Move up a tier only after
delivering the tier below successfully at least twice.

| Tier | Pilot build | Retainer | You're here when |
|---|---|---|---|
| Starting out | $800 – $1,500 | $800 – $1,500/mo | No client case studies yet |
| Proven | $2,000 – $4,000 | $2,000 – $3,500/mo | 2–3 delivered projects, written testimonials |
| Specialist | $5,000 – $12,000 | $4,000 – $8,000/mo | Known for one vertical, inbound enquiries |

Raise prices when you're turning work away, when three prospects in a row accept without negotiating,
or when you finish a project in half the estimated time. All three mean you're underpriced.

## What each of the five systems is worth

Anchored on what the manual alternative costs, which is the only number that matters in the room.

| System | Manual cost it removes | Pilot price | Retainer angle |
|---|---|---|---|
| **LeadFlow AI** | Lost deals from slow response — usually the largest number on the page | $2,000 – $5,000 | Scoring tuned monthly against actual conversion data |
| **Concierge Agent** | ~1 support FTE, plus after-hours enquiries lost entirely | $2,500 – $6,000 | Per-tenant hosting + monthly content-gap report |
| **DocuFlow** | Bookkeeping hours + duplicate payments | $2,500 – $6,000 | Per-document pricing above an included volume |
| **Outreach Engine** | An SDR's research time; a burned sending domain | $1,500 – $4,000 | Managed campaigns, monthly |
| **ContentForge** | 10–20 hours of repurposing per month | $1,500 – $3,500 | Per-brand-voice licence, monthly |

Agencies reselling you white-label will pay toward the lower end but bring volume and no sales cost —
often the better deal in year one.

## Structures that work

**Fixed-price pilot** — the default. One workflow, scoped tightly, 50/50 payment. Low risk for them,
predictable for you, and it produces the case study.

**Monthly retainer** — the actual goal. Predictable income, compounding client knowledge, no constant
re-selling. Convert every successful pilot: *"Most clients keep me on monthly for changes and
monitoring — want me to send what that looks like?"*

**Per-unit pricing** — powerful where volume is visible. "$0.40 per invoice processed, minimum $600/month"
sounds cheap next to $2 of manual labour per invoice, and it scales with their growth automatically.

**Performance component** — only with clients you trust and metrics you can actually see. A reduced
base plus a share of measurable savings can pay very well, but never take it as the whole fee.

---

## Rules

**Always take a deposit.** 50% upfront, no exceptions, including for people who seem trustworthy.
Anyone who refuses to pay a deposit is telling you something useful.

**Discount by cutting scope, never by cutting the number.** *"I can get to $1,800 if we drop the
dashboard and ship the pipeline plus CSV export first."* Straight discounts teach the client that your
prices are fiction.

**Charge for the audit once you're busy.** Free audits are a customer-acquisition cost that makes sense
while you're building a client base. When you're at capacity, $300 credited against the build filters
out everyone who was never going to buy.

**Bill in USD.** Both UAE and US clients are comfortable with it, and it protects you from currency
movement. Wise or Payoneer for receiving; put the payment terms in the proposal, not in a later email.

**Raise prices on new clients only.** Never on existing ones mid-engagement — grandfather them and let
the new rate apply to new work. The goodwill is worth more than the difference.

---

## Estimating without underquoting

Break the build into concrete pieces, estimate each, then multiply the total by 1.5. That multiplier is
not padding — it's integration surprises, the client's data being messier than described, review cycles,
and the two or three days of "one small change" that follow every handover.

Then sanity-check against the value: if the estimate lands at $1,200 for something saving them $4,000 a
month, the estimate is wrong, not the value. Charge closer to the value.
